# 10 — Audit of the audio and network paths, 2026-08-29

Asked for after two testers found the port unusable in ways the 1.x client is not: a
thirty-second wait before anyone could be heard, two to three seconds of delay, and a peer
whose ring kept flickering. Those three are fixed. The question this document answers is
the one that follows: **what else is like that, and are we anywhere worse than the client
we are replacing?**

## How it was done

Forty-one agents over eight dimensions — NAT/TURN/ICE, unwired code, the audio receive
path, the audio send path, timing and cadence, signalling and peer lifecycle, the per-frame
voice decision, and concurrency. Every finding was then handed to a separate sceptic
instructed to **refute** it, told to default to refused, and required to produce a concrete
reproduction before accepting. Twenty-nine survived; three were refuted and are recorded at
the end so they are not raised again. A completeness critic then named what nobody had
opened and audited three of those gaps itself.

Thirty-two findings stand. They are not thirty-two independent mistakes: five of them are
one root cause seen from five directions, and that root cause is the largest item here.

## The four questions, answered

**Are there more bugs in the audio path?** Yes, and two of them stop the client dead: one
aborts the process, one makes every voice unintelligible. Both are ordinary hardware, not
exotic.

**Are there more bugs in the network path, particularly NAT and TURN?** Yes, and this is
the worst area. The relay fallback that this project wrote, tested and documented does not
reach the network at all — not because it is unwired, but because the transport crate
throws it away one layer below, silently.

**Are we worse than the Node client anywhere?** Yes, in eleven places. The largest is that
**nothing in the Rust client repairs a broken connection.** The Node client restarts ICE,
rebuilds on failure, escalates to the relay on evidence, and retries forever. We do none of
it, and the code to do all of it is written, tested, and never called.

**Are there more "present but not connected" situations?** Yes — nine. Marked ⚡ below.

**Are there more timing problems?** Yes, five. The same class as the five fixed yesterday:
a module assuming another module's calling rate, with the assumption written in a comment
instead of a type.

## A. It crashes, or it is unintelligible

### A1 ⚠ The sensitivity slider kills the client, and keeps killing it

`crates/acl-audio/src/vad.rs:181` · `crates/acl-client/src/audio.rs:107`

`finish_calibration` calls `f64::clamp(min_noise_level, max_noise_level)`. The floor comes
from the player's stored `micSensitivity`; the ceiling is always `MAX_NOISE_LEVEL = 0.7`.
`f64::clamp` panics when `min > max`. `Floor::settings` writes the floor and never touches
the ceiling.

The slider is inverted in the UI, so the six positions that do this are the six at the
**least sensitive** end — exactly where a player goes when the microphone keeps opening on
their fan. Release builds are `panic = "abort"`, and the panic is inside the cpal capture
callback: the window vanishes with no message, about a second after the slider stops
moving.

Then the worse half. The value is already saved. Every subsequent launch aborts a second
after the microphone opens — long enough to show the main window, not long enough to reach
Settings and drag it back. **The client is dead until `settings.json` is edited by hand.**

Reproduce without touching a slider: set `"micSensitivityEnabled": true, "micSensitivity":
0.75` and start the client.

### A2 ⚠ A speaker that is not stereo plays everyone at 2–4× speed

`crates/acl-client/src/audio.rs:623`

The output callback writes the mixer's interleaved stereo one sample per slot and never
consults the device's channel count:

```rust
for slot in buffer.iter_mut() { let Some(sample) = ready.pop_front() else { break }; *slot = sample; }
```

`at_any_rate` asks for two channels and stores whatever came back. cpal's WASAPI backend
reports exactly one channel count per device — the mix format's — so a 5.1 endpoint returns
6 and a Bluetooth hands-free endpoint returns 1. On 5.1 the mix is consumed three times too
fast; on 7.1, four times.

The repository already has this failure written down, for the test tone, at
`audio.rs:526`: *"Pushed once, the tone played across the two channels at double speed and
half the length."* The same mistake is in the voice path. There is also no output
resampler, so a device that does not offer 48 kHz plays at the wrong pitch.

### A3 A microphone that will not open takes the speaker with it

`crates/acl-client/src/audio.rs:578`

`start` opens the microphone first and returns `Err` on failure, so the speaker is never
opened. A player whose microphone is busy, missing, or at an unsupported rate hears
**nobody** — rather than being unable to speak while still hearing the lobby, which is what
1.x does. `Audio` already carries a `trouble: Option<String>` for precisely this.

## B. Nothing repairs itself

This is the largest finding in the document, and the one the 1.x client explicitly fixed.
Its own comment, at `src/renderer/Voice.tsx:1324`, describes what we now do:

> A connection that fails is gone for the rest of the session: new ones are only created
> when a socket joins the lobby or sends an offer, and a peer that is already in the lobby
> does neither. That is why a player who drops out of one conversation stays out of it
> until the app is restarted, while everyone else still hears them.

### B1 ⚠⚡ Nothing acts on `Failed`

`crates/acl-client/src/net.rs:860` · `crates/acl-net/src/reconnect.rs` (whole module)

`drain` logs the state and reports `connected: false` to the ring. That is all. The whole
of `acl_net::reconnect` — `initiates_reconnect`, `reconnect_delay`, `should_give_up`,
`BASE_DELAY`, `MAX_DELAY`, `ANSWER_GRACE`, `SLOW_DELAY` — has **zero production callers**.

A wifi roam, a NAT rebind, or a relay reservation expiring silences that pair for the rest
of the session, while everyone else still hears both of them.

### B2 ⚡ ICE restart is implemented and never called

`crates/acl-net/src/peer.rs:284`

`RepairPolicy::poll` decides between `RestartIce` and `Rebuild`, with both non-obvious
rules written down — only the initiator restarts, one restart per connection. Zero callers.
`restart_ice()` appears nowhere. `src/renderer/peer.ts:153` does it.

This is the cheap repair: a re-gather that keeps the connection, its tracks and its DTLS
session. Without it every recoverable blip has to become a rebuild — and there are no
rebuilds either.

### B3 ⚡ Relay escalation could not run even if it were wired

`crates/acl-net/src/reconnect.rs:135`

`should_use_relay` needs `RelaySignals { attempt, relay_candidates, other_peers_needed_relay }`.
Nothing in the Rust workspace ever counts relay candidates or observes an ICE candidate
error. `peer.ts:275` counts them; `peer.ts:289` reads RFC 5766's **486, Allocation Quota
Reached** — the one code that separates *"the relay works and is full"* from *"this machine
cannot reach the relay"*, which is the difference between escalating and making things
worse. Neither exists here.

A player behind symmetric or carrier-grade NAT is unreachable on the Rust client and
reachable on the Electron one.

### B4 ⚠ A dropped websocket is permanent

`crates/acl-client/src/net.rs:640` · `crates/acl-client/src/main.rs:1865`

On `Ok(None)` the worker parks on `orders.recv()` and waits forever. `keep_connected`
treats `State::Failed` as terminal. A server restart, or three seconds of unplugged
ethernet, ends voice for the rest of the process — the lobby list still paints, so nothing
looks wrong. The one manual reconnect path does not re-emit `join`, so it reconnects to the
server and not to the lobby.

### B5 The heartbeat deadline can never be checked

`crates/acl-client/src/net.rs:634`

The worker wraps `connection.next()` in a 50 ms timeout. The Engine.IO tick is a 1 s
internal sleep inside that future. The timeout always fires first, so the deadline check is
never reached: **a socket that has stopped answering is indistinguishable from a quiet
one.** A timing bug of exactly the class fixed yesterday.

### B6 Offer glare is a deadlock with no way out

`crates/acl-core/src/peers.rs:388`

Two players joining within the same server tick both see `Arrival::Newcomer` and both offer.
Each then routes the other's offer to `Create`, **throws away its own half-built
connection**, and answers the other's. Each then receives an answer for the connection it
just destroyed and applies it to one already in `stable`; the `rtc` crate returns `Err`, and
`net.rs:825` discards it with no log. Both survivors carry the other's dead credentials, so
ICE can never complete.

`Arrival::Newcomer` is a convention that holds while the server's ordering holds — not a
tie-break. `initiates_reconnect` is the tie-break, and it is not called.

## C. NAT and TURN

### C1 ⚠ The transport throws away every TURN-over-TCP and `turns:` URL, silently

`crates/acl-net/src/ice.rs:99` · `webrtc =0.20.3`

`with_tcp_relays` exists because *"a player on a network that blocks outbound UDP … cannot
reach that relay at all"*. It has eight tests. It is correct. And
`webrtc-0.20.3/src/peer_connection/transports/turn_relayer.rs:250`:

```rust
if url.is_secure() { warn!("Skipping unsupported secure TURN url {}", url); continue; }
if url.proto.to_string() != "udp" { warn!("Skipping unsupported non-UDP TURN url {}", url); continue; }
```

Every `?transport=tcp` entry and every `turns:` entry is discarded before it allocates.
Only plain UDP `turn:` survives.

It compounds three ways. `peers.rs:510` builds every connection with
`.with_udp_addrs(vec!["0.0.0.0:0"])` and never calls `with_tcp_addrs`, so there are no
ICE-TCP host candidates either — **the client is UDP-only on every candidate type there
is.** And no `log` implementation is installed anywhere in the workspace, so both `warn!`
calls go to the no-op logger: the player gets no diagnostic at all.

Our own server advertises a bare UDP `turn:` alongside the TCP form, so this is invisible
to everyone whose network permits UDP. For everyone whose network does not — schools,
corporate guest wifi, some carriers, the population TURN exists for — it is total,
permanent and silent. Chromium allocates over both. **The same player is audible on 1.x and
silent on 2.x.**

There is a second, narrower case: a server advertising only `turns:` with `forceRelayOnly`
is accepted by `apply_client_peer_config` (because `has_relay` counts `turns:`), the policy
becomes `Relay`, host and reflexive gathering are suppressed — and the sole relay is
skipped. Zero candidates of any kind.

## D. The lobby you left

### D1 Leaving a lobby closes no peer connection, and poisons the next one

`crates/acl-client/src/net.rs:964`

`Command::Leave` calls only `live.leave()`; `obey` does not receive the mesh at all.
`Session::leave` clears the membership and **discards the `Action::Disconnect` list that
`Membership::clear` returns** — the one call site that throws it away, where the other two
membership mutations turn it into `Event::PeerLeft` and thus into `mesh.close`.

You do not end up in two lobbies at once: unplaced peers are mixed at zero gain, and the
peers who stayed behind receive `left` and close their side. The damage is to the **next**
lobby. `PeerSet::offer` is deliberately non-destructive — `if !self.holds(peer) { build }` —
and nothing removes a peer on a failed state. Meet the same person in the next lobby and
the offer is made on the dead connection, marked as a renegotiation, against credentials
that no longer exist. That pair never connects again for the life of the app.

### D2 The `id` event is never emitted

`crates/acl-client/src/main.rs:1932`

`follow_the_lobby` sends `join` once per lobby-code change, carrying whatever the reader
could see at that instant — `-1` when the local player record is not yet readable, which is
the normal case, because the game state leaves `Menu` before the player record appears. The
`id` event exists in the protocol to correct exactly this and is never sent. A player who
crossed that edge early is identified as `-1` to the whole lobby and inaudible to all of it.

## E. Audio quality and pacing

### E1 ⚡ Forward error correction is switched on and never fed

`crates/acl-audio/src/codec.rs:130` · `crates/acl-audio/src/fec.rs:74`

`set_inband_fec(true)`, then `set_packet_loss_perc(0)`. `fec.rs:8` says why that is nothing:
*libopus spends bits on redundancy in proportion to the packet loss percentage.*
`set_packet_loss` and the whole `FecController` have only test callers. The receive side
dutifully calls `decode_lost` looking for redundancy the send side never puts in. Every lost
frame falls through to plain concealment.

### E2 A dry jitter buffer clicks instead of concealing

`crates/acl-audio/src/jitter.rs:315`

The empty-buffer branch emits digital silence. Opus's concealment is right there in the same
file and is called on the *lost packet* path but not the *starved* path. A hard edge to zero
is a click; concealment decays to silence on its own.

### E3 Outgoing audio leaves in 200 ms bursts

`crates/acl-client/src/main.rs:1597`, `main.rs:1550`

Both directions of the audio transport are still drained from the eframe update loop, whose
floor is 200 ms when the pointer is not over the window — which is the whole time anyone is
actually playing. Ten 20 ms frames leave at once, ten times a second. This is the structural
fix identified yesterday and not yet made.

### E4 Push-to-talk is applied at 5 Hz to already-encoded frames

`crates/acl-client/src/main.rs:1573`

The gate is checked when the batch is drained, not when the audio is captured. Releasing the
key cuts up to 200 ms off the tail of the word; pressing it sends up to 200 ms of what was
already in the buffer before the press.

### E5 ⚡ The whole send-side DSP runs inside the capture callback

`crates/acl-client/src/audio.rs:803`

The echo canceller, a 1024-point FFT, the VAD and the Opus encoder all run inside cpal's
real-time callback, with four `Vec` allocations per 20 ms frame. `acl_audio::ring::Ring` —
the callback/worker seam written for exactly this, 308 lines, tested — has zero production
callers. This is also why A1 is fatal rather than an error message: the panic happens on the
audio thread.

## F. Game-rule parity

### F1 The hearing-range cutoff ignores vision hearing

`crates/acl-client/src/main.rs:1704`

`voice_params` is handed `lobby.max_distance` where the panner is handed `hearing_range`.
With the host's vision-hearing toggle on, the two disagree: a player is shown as in range
and cut to silence, or heard past the cutoff.

### F2 The stereo image is not 1.x's

`crates/acl-client/src/main.rs:1761`

The game's `y` is mapped to the panner's depth instead of its elevation, and the fixed
`z = -0.5` the Electron client uses is dropped. Someone above you on the map is panned as
though they were behind you, and the distance loses its 0.5 floor.

### F3 The host's lobby settings reach nobody

`crates/acl-client/src/main.rs:1619`

Every client applies its own local rules. The host turning on walls-block-audio or haunting
changes nothing for anyone else — and there is no event carrying them, the way
`impostorRadio` has one.

### F4 The meeting overlay computes every seat and draws none of them

`crates/acl-client/src/main.rs:1457`

`let talking = false;` — hard-coded, above the loop that decides what to draw.

### F5 Impostor radio beats a vent

`crates/acl-audio/src/voice.rs:378`

In `Voice.tsx` the vent branch runs last and overwrites the radio muffle. Here the radio
wins. Minor, and audible to two impostors.

## G. Settings that do nothing

### G1 ⚡ The device pickers

`crates/acl-client/src/audio.rs:606`

Microphone and speaker are chosen in Settings, stored, and never read. The client always
opens the Windows default. A player on a USB headset picks it, sees it selected, and talks
into their laptop's array microphone.

### G2 The public-lobby listing can go stale

`crates/acl-client/src/main.rs:1848`

`self.listed` compares the payload without the lobby code and is never cleared, so a host's
second lobby of a session can be absent from the browser.

## Refuted — do not raise these again

- **`audio.rs:1291`** A rebuilt peer restarting its RTP sequence causing minutes of
  concealment. The buffer re-primes; it does not.
- **`transport.rs:112`** The 50 ms timeout cutting `Connection::next` mid-send and dropping
  a batch. The send is not cancellable at that point.
- **`net.rs:686`** `PeerSet`'s inbox filling unboundedly after the session ends. It is
  drained.

## Where nobody looked

Named by the completeness critic, for the next pass rather than this one: the whole of
**`acl-game`** (~4,300 lines — the port of the file CLAUDE.md calls the most fragile in the
project), **`acl-ipc`** and **`acl-helper`** (the transport carrying every game frame),
`acl-core/src/link.rs`, and the effects half of the mix graph (`reverb.rs`, `convolver.rs`,
`fft.rs`, `biquad.rs`). `apm.rs` and `resample.rs` landed the day before this audit;
`resample.rs` was read in full and is correct.

Roughly forty further findings were produced and not verified, because verification was
capped at the four most severe per dimension. They are listed verbatim in the run log rather
than dropped silently.

### C1 is a dependency's limitation, not this repository's

`webrtc =0.20.3` discards every TURN-over-TCP and every `turns:` URL before it allocates,
and it cannot be fixed here. What was fixed is that it was **silent**: no crate in this
workspace installed an implementation of the `log` facade, so the transport's own warning
went nowhere. It reaches the log file now, `ice::usable_relay` answers what the transport
can actually allocate through rather than what the server advertised, and a lobby whose
server offers only TCP or TLS relays says so in a sentence a person can read.

### E5 wants a listening test

Every gate that can be run has been. No test in this repository opens a real microphone, so
the proof that the capture path still sounds right is somebody speaking into one.

## Order of work

1. **A1** — it bricks the installation. One line.
2. **A2, A3** — ordinary hardware, total failure.
3. **B1–B4** — the repair layer. Mostly wiring code that already exists and is tested.
4. **D1** — small, and it corrupts the next lobby.
5. **E3, E4, E5** — take the audio path off the repaint loop, which also carries E4 with it.
6. **C1** — the largest, because it is upstream. Either TURN-over-TCP support, or stop
   claiming a fallback we do not have, loudly.
7. **E1, E2, F1–F5, G1, G2** — quality and parity.
